Every app is reachable at a free `*.symplax.app` subdomain with HTTPS. You can add your own domain on top of it, and Symplax gets a Let's Encrypt certificate for it once your DNS points at your server.

## The free subdomain

When you deploy an app, Symplax gives it an address made from the app name, such as `billing-api.symplax.app`. If that name is taken, it adds a short suffix, such as `billing-api-3f9c.symplax.app`.

- HTTPS works straight away, with a certificate Symplax provides for `*.symplax.app`.
- Plain HTTP requests are redirected to HTTPS.
- The subdomain is kept for the life of the app, even after you add a custom domain. Both names keep working.

You never need to edit DNS for the subdomain.

## Add a custom domain

<Warning>
  Symplax does not manage your DNS. You create the record at your domain registrar or DNS provider, such as Cloudflare, Namecheap or Route 53.
</Warning>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter the domain">
    Open your app, go to **Settings**, click **Add domain** under **Domains**, type your domain, for example `example.com`, and click **Add**. You can also set it in the **Domain** field when you first deploy.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create an A record">
    Until the domain is set up, its row shows the record to create. It is an **A** record whose value is your server's IP address.

    | Type | Name | Value |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `A` | `@` | `203.0.113.10` (your server's IP) |

    The name is `@` for the root of a domain, such as `example.com`, and the part before it for anything else, such as `app` for `app.example.com`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for the certificate">
    Click **Refresh** to see whether the domain points at your server. Once it does, Symplax requests a Let's Encrypt certificate for you, usually within a couple of minutes. The row then shows **Valid Configuration**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Use an A record, not a CNAME. The certificate is issued on your server, so the domain has to reach your server directly. If you use Cloudflare, set the record to **DNS only** (grey cloud) so traffic is not proxied through Cloudflare.
</Note>

## www and the root domain

When your domain is the root of a site, such as `example.com`, Symplax also answers on `www.example.com` and redirects it to `example.com` with a permanent (301) redirect that keeps the page and query. If your domain is `www.example.com`, the root `example.com` redirects to it instead.

To use it, create a second A record:

| Type | Name | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `A` | `www` | `203.0.113.10` (your server's IP) |

- The redirect gets its own certificate, so your main domain works whether or not this record exists. Until you add it, the www row shows as not set up.
- To turn it off, click **Edit** on the www row, then **Remove**. **Add redirect** turns it back on.
- Subdomains such as `app.example.com` do not get a www redirect.
- It needs agent 2.1.53 or later on your server. On an older agent, the www row asks you to update it from the server's **Settings**.

## How SSL works

Certificates for custom domains come from Let's Encrypt, issued by certbot on your server and installed into nginx.

- **When you save a domain**, Symplax updates nginx and requests a certificate straight away.
- **If DNS was not ready**, which is common when a domain still points at your old host, Symplax keeps checking whether the domain now resolves to your server: every minute for the first hour after you add it, then every ten minutes. When it does, it requests the certificate for you, with no redeploy needed. After a failed attempt it waits 15 minutes in that first hour and an hour after that, to stay within Let's Encrypt's rate limits.
- **If something else is in the way**, the Domains card says so: a CAA record that does not allow Let's Encrypt, an IPv6 (AAAA) record pointing elsewhere, or the reason the last attempt failed.
- **Renewal** is handled by certbot's scheduled renewal on your server.

Each row tells you what its name resolves to today:

- It points at this server: the certificate is on its way, or already working.
- It points somewhere else: Symplax shows the address it found. Change the record to the one shown.
- It does not resolve yet: add the record, then allow a few minutes for it to spread.

## Your app must accept its domain

Some frameworks reject requests for hostnames they do not know: Django's `ALLOWED_HOSTS`, Rails' `config.hosts`, Sinatra's host authorization. Before going live, Symplax sends one request with your domain as the `Host` header. If your app accepted the health check but rejects its own domain, the deploy fails with a message telling you which setting to change, instead of going live and blocking every visitor.

Add both your custom domain and your `*.symplax.app` subdomain to that allow-list.

## Limits

- An app has one custom domain, plus its www redirect and its free subdomain.
- Traffic reaches your app through nginx on your server, which listens on ports 80 and 443. Let's Encrypt validates your domain over port 80.
