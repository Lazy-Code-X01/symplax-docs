---
title: "Uptime monitoring"
description: "Symplax checks every deployed app's public URL every minute and emails you when it goes down and when it recovers."
---

Symplax checks every deployed app from the outside, the way a visitor would. Once a minute it sends a request to the app's public HTTPS address, through DNS, nginx and SSL. That catches problems the deploy health check cannot see, such as an expired domain, a broken nginx config or an app that started fine and then began returning errors.

There is nothing to set up. It is on for every app with a domain, on every plan.

## What is checked

Each minute, Symplax requests:

```text
https://<your app's domain><health check path>
```

The domain is your custom domain if you have one, otherwise the free `*.symplax.app` subdomain. The path is the **Health Check** path from your app's **Settings**, or `/` if you have not set one. Each request times out after 10 seconds.

## What counts as down

| Response | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Any status below 500, including `301`, `401`, `403` and `404` | Up |
| A `5xx` status | Down |
| No response: DNS failure, refused connection, TLS error or timeout | Down |

A `404` or a `401` means your app received the request and answered, which is what is being measured. Redirects are not followed, because a redirect is your app answering.

## When you get an email

Symplax emails the person who created the app:

- **When the app goes down**: after **three failed checks in a row**, about three minutes. The email says what failed, for example the HTTP status or the connection error.
- **When it recovers**: on the first successful check after it was down. The email says how long it was down.

You get one email for each change, not one every minute while the app stays down.

While an app is down, its status in the dashboard reads **Not responding**, even though its container may still be running.

## Rules that avoid false alarms

Symplax skips an app, instead of calling it down, when:

- **It has no domain.** There is nothing to request from outside.
- **It is not deployed.** A failed or pending app is not expected to answer.
- **A deploy is running.** The container is being replaced, and a brief error is expected.
- **Its server is offline.** The app is not down, its machine is, and one outage should not become one email per app. The app's uptime status shows as unknown.

A single failed check never sends an email, so a restart or one dropped request does not wake you up. A new app does not show as down on its first slow minute either: it stays unknown until it passes or reaches three failures.

## Turn the emails off

Uptime emails follow the **Deployment Updates** switch under **Notification Preferences** in **Settings**, on the **Profile** tab. Turning that off also stops uptime alerts.
